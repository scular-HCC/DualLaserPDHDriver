01

System Overview

The design is a dual-channel CW laser and TEC driver for 1550 nm DFB butterfly modules. The top-level KiCad sheet describes it as: “Dual Channel CW Laser + TEC Driver — ML20550KK14Z600L” with “1550nm 70mW DFB butterfly | Bipolar TEC H-bridge -1.5A/+2.5A | AD5064 16-bit DAC”.

Each channel provides:

  • ACC laser current driver with Kelvin sense (R5 = 0.25 Ω, INA826 readback G=12).
  • Bipolar TEC H-bridge (IRF9540N + IRLB8721) with sense resistor R7 = 0.1 Ω and INA826 G=20.
  • NTC thermistor readback via 10 kΩ divider into Teensy ADC.
  • Optional dither injection from an AD9833 DDS into the laser error amplifier summing node.

±15 V Tracking Implementation

The “±15V.kicad_sch” sheet uses a TPS7A39 dual LDO to generate ±16.5 V from the ±24 V rectified rails, followed by LT3045/LT3094 to produce ±15 V. The comment notes: “RAIL 1: +/-15V direct to LT1028 op-amps”.

Key idea: both positive and negative outputs share the same internal reference. Matching feedback ratios on FBP and FBN guarantee that +V and −V track in magnitude during startup, shutdown, and load transients.

TEC H-Bridge Drivers

The “TEC Control.kicad_sch” sheet implements bipolar TEC control using an OPA192 error amp, IRF9540N P-channel high-side FETs, IRLB8721 N-channel low-side FETs, and a 0.1 Ω Kelvin sense resistor. The top-level notes: “TEC bipolar H-bridge: -1.5A to +2.5A, ±3.8V max”.

Readback & Lock-in Signal Path

The “Readback.kicad_sch” sheet is annotated: “READBACK + LOCK-IN SIGNAL PATH | 4x INA826 InAmp | NTC dividers | OPA380 TIA | OPA2134 AAF”. Two external photodiode channels feed OPA380 TIAs, followed by 2-pole 30 kHz Butterworth anti-alias filters (OPA2134) into Teensy ADC1/ADC2 at 100 kS/s for digital lock-in.

Dither Injection

The sheet notes: “DITHER INJECTION — AC couple + series R sets dither amplitude at laser error amp IN+ summing node”. Calculation comment: “V_AD9833=0.6Vpk, R_dith=383k, R2=1k -> V_sum=0.6*1k/(383k+1k)=1.56mV”.

Lock-in design target: “Dither amplitude: 0.32mA peak -> 96MHz FM excursion -> 0.1x HWHM of Q=1e5 ring resonator at 1550nm”.

Design Rationale

Why Linear TEC & Laser Drivers?

  • Switching noise from PWM TEC drivers couples into the laser cavity via temperature and supply rails; linear H-bridges avoid this.
  • Linear ACC drivers with Kelvin sense provide predictable, low-noise current with minimal EMI, critical for lock-in detection around a high-Q ring resonator.

Why TPS7A39 + LT304x?

  • TPS7A39 guarantees matched positive and negative rails using a single internal reference and matched feedback ratios.
  • LT3045/LT3094 add ultra-low noise and PSRR, isolating analog loops from rectifier ripple and digital activity.

Why External 5 V Reference?

  • LTC6655-5 offers 0.25 ppm/°C drift and very low noise, improving DAC linearity and long-term stability.
  • Separating DAC Vref from digital 5 V (LM7805) avoids coupling MCU noise into the laser and TEC set-points.

Why 47 Ω Series Protection on Butterfly Pins?

  • Limits inrush and fault currents into delicate laser and TEC structures during hot-plug or miswiring.
  • Provides modest damping for any cable inductance between board and package.
  • For ground pins, consider replacing with direct connections to AGND to avoid ground bounce; keep 47 Ω on signal and supply pins.
