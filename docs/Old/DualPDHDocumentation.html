1. System Overview

The DualLaserPDHDriver board implements a dual-laser Pound–Drever–Hall (PDH) driver for two closely spaced 1550 nm distributed feedback (DFB) lasers. The system is intended to lock both lasers to a high-Q (≈10⁻⁵) optical reference with a frequency separation of less than 20 GHz.

1.1 High-Level Functional Blocks

  • Laser Driver Interfaces – Current/temperature control interfaces for two 1550 nm DFB lasers.
  • RF Modulation Path – Generates phase/frequency modulation for PDH sidebands.
  • Optical Detection & PDH Demodulation – Photodiode front-end, RF amplification, mixer/demodulator.
  • Loop Filter / Control Output – Analog control signals back to laser drivers (current, piezo, or temperature).
  • Reference & Timing – Local oscillator (LO) and any reference clocking used for PDH demodulation.
  • Power Management – Input power, regulation, and distribution to analog/RF/digital domains.

1.2 Key Performance Targets

  • Wavelength: 1550 nm DFB lasers (two channels).
  • Optical reference Q-factor: ≈ 10⁻⁵ (application-dependent).
  • Laser frequency spacing: < 20 GHz.
  • Low-noise analog and RF paths suitable for PDH locking.

3. Signal Flow

This section describes the PDH signal flow from RF generation through optical interaction to the error signal and control outputs. The SVG below focuses on the logical signal path rather than physical placement.

Figure 2 – PDH signal flow from RF generation to error signal and feedback to the lasers.

3.1 Narrative Signal Flow Description

  1. The RF local oscillator (LO) generates the PDH modulation frequency \( f_{\text{PDH}} \).
  2. This RF signal modulates the laser(s), creating sidebands around the carrier frequency.
  3. The modulated light interacts with the optical cavity; the reflected/transmitted light encodes cavity detuning.
  4. A photodiode converts the optical signal to an electrical RF signal, which is amplified.
  5. The RF photocurrent is mixed with the LO (or a phase-shifted version) to produce the PDH error signal.
  6. The error signal passes through a loop filter (e.g., PID), shaping the control bandwidth and stability.
  7. The filtered control signal drives the laser actuators (current, TEC, PZT), closing the frequency lock loop.

5. Design Rationale

5.1 Architecture Choices

  • Dual-laser architecture: Supports two 1550 nm DFB lasers with < 20 GHz spacing, enabling differential or multi-channel locking schemes.
  • PDH locking: Chosen for its high sensitivity to frequency detuning and compatibility with high-Q optical references.
  • Analog RF demodulation: Mixer-based PDH demodulation avoids ADC noise and latency, providing wide bandwidth control.
  • Separated power domains: RF, analog, and digital rails are separated to minimize coupling and noise injection into the error signal.

5.2 Component-Level Considerations

  • Photodiode & TIA: Selected for bandwidth compatible with the PDH modulation frequency and cavity linewidth.
  • Mixer / Demodulator: Chosen for low conversion loss and good isolation between LO and IF ports.
  • Loop filter topology: PID or lead-lag network tuned for stability margin and desired lock bandwidth.
  • Connectors & pinouts: Arranged to keep RF paths short and to separate sensitive analog nodes from noisy digital/control lines.

5.3 Mechanical & Layout Considerations

  • RF traces routed with controlled impedance where necessary.
  • Ground planes used to reduce loop area and improve shielding.
  • Critical analog nodes kept away from board edges and connectors.
  • Provision for mounting near the optical setup (e.g., compact form factor, connector orientation).
