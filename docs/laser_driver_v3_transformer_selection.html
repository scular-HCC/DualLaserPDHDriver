01

Two-Transformer Architecture — Why the TEC Rail Is Separate

pm6V_TEC.kicad_sch
Architecture ±12Vac net feeds only the TEC supply — a second secondary/transformer +12Vac / −12Vac dangling net ▶

The global labels +12Vac / −12Vac appear only in pm6V_TEC.kicad_sch with no counterpart on the mains sheet — a dangling net. The ±6V TEC H-bridge supply was always intended to run from its own winding, distinct from the 18Vac winding that feeds the ±15V precision rails. Three reasons make this the correct topology:

ReasonDetail
Bidirectional loadThe TEC drive is a bipolar linear H-bridge needing symmetric ± rails; a dedicated CT winding is the natural fit.
Noise isolationTEC current is high-ripple; its own rectifier + reservoir keeps that noise off the ±15V/±18V rails feeding the precision analog front-end.
Dropout / dissipationRegulating ±6V down from the 18Vac winding (~±24V raw) would waste enormous power in the linear pass devices. A lower, dedicated secondary keeps dissipation sane.
⚠ No single catalog toroid does both. Every stock toroidal on DigiKey has identical dual secondaries (configurable parallel/series), not a mixed 18V + 12V output. A combined 18V/12V part would be custom-wound. Two separate transformers is the right call — and both are now mounted off the signal board (see Section 08).

Decision · H-bridge supply voltage
With a TEC that maxes at 4.7 V, the ±6 V rails already give ~1.3 V of headroom for the linear pass devices. Raising the supply to 9–12 V (as the schematic note speculated) would only dump more power into the pass transistors as heat for zero benefit. Keep ±6 V; strike the "increase to 9–12V" note. If anything the AC secondary should come down — see Section 06.
✓ Duty cycle clarified: Both channels at full 2.5 A cooling simultaneously occurs only as a brief power-on transient (~1–2 s), never sustained. Sustained load is the steady-state hold current (~1–2 A) plus occasional cooldown ramps (~3 A). This sets the thermal sizing at ~3 A with a short 5 A peak — not 5 A continuous.

Dropout ≈ 2.0 V Emitter-follower headroom = VBE + IB·Rbase V_CE(sat) non-binding ▶

The pass stage is an emitter follower: the output sits one VBE below the clamped base reference, and the device must stay out of saturation (VCE > VCE(sat)). The binding term is the base drive, not saturation. Values read from the datasheet curves at VCE = 5 V (not the 8 A spec point):

05

Bulk Capacitor — The Binding Constraint

pm6V_TEC.kicad_sch · C41 / C42
⚠ Under-sized 2200 µF reservoir → ~11 V pp ripple at 3 A → trough collapses raise to ~10,000 µF ▶

The cap multiplier keeps regulating only while the raw rail's ripple trough stays above Vout + dropout = 6 + 2 = 8 V. Ripple on the reservoir is Vpp = I / (f·C), f = 120 Hz. With the present C41/C42 = 2200 µF the trough falls far below 8 V — the supply cannot deliver 3 A cleanly at any sane secondary voltage.

Vpp = I / (f · C) f = 120 Hz (full-wave)
2200 µF @ 3A: 3 / (120 × 0.0022) = 11.4 V pp → trough collapses ✗
4700 µF @ 3A: 3 / (120 × 0.0047) = 5.3 V pp → still marginal
10,000 µF @ 3A: 3 / (120 × 0.010) = 2.5 V pp → trough OK ✓
10,000 µF @ 5A: 5 / (120 × 0.010) = 4.2 V pp → brief power-on dip
15,000 µF @ 5A: 5 / (120 × 0.015) = 2.8 V pp → clean even at 5A ✓
Reservoir (per rail)Ripple @3ATrough @9Vac (peak 11.7V)Result
2200 µF (current)11.4 V pp~0.3 V✗ drops out

⚠ Critical action — independent of the transformer choice: raise the TEC raw reservoir from 2200 µF to ~10,000 µF (or 15,000 µF to ride through the full 5 A power-on transient without output ripple). At a 9 Vac secondary a 25 V rating is sufficient for these caps.
06

Secondary Voltage — 9 Vac

pm6V_TEC.kicad_sch
9 Vac (was 12) Halves pass-transistor dissipation while holding regulation 13.5 W vs 26 W @ 3A ▶

With the reservoir fixed at ~10,000 µF, the only remaining freedom is the secondary voltage. Lower secondary → less linear drop → less heat, bounded below by the need to keep the trough ≥ 8 V. The headroom budget gives the minimum:

Vsec ≥ (Vout + Vdrop + Vdiode + Vripple,pp) / √2
= (6 + 2 + 1 + 2.5) / 1.414 = ~8.1 V rms → 9 Vac (nearest standard, with margin)
7.5 Vac gets too tight once the 5 A transient is included; do not go lower.
SecondaryPeak rawAvg raw @3APass diss. @3AVerdict
12 Vac (original)~16.0 V~14.7 V~26 Whot + needs big cap anyway
9 Vac (recommended)~11.7 V~10.5 V~13.5 W✓ balanced
7.5 Vac~9.6 V~8.5 V~7.5 Wtoo tight at 5A peak
✓ At steady-state hold (~1 A) the pass dissipation at 9 Vac is only ~5 W. The 13.5 W figure is the ~3 A cooldown case; the 5 A peak (~22 W) lasts a second or two. With RθJC = 0.7 °C/W the MJL pair on a modest chassis heatsink handles this comfortably.

TEC Transformer — Sizing & Part Selection (Chassis)

DigiKey · Talema 700xxK series
Chassis · 62081 50 VA, 2×9V CT toroid — chassis-mounted, secondary to board 2.78 A/winding ▶
Isec,rms ≈ 1.7 × IDC = 1.7 × 3 A = ~5.1 A (cap-input full-wave form factor)
Sustained load (hold ~1–2 A) well under 2.78 A/winding; 5 A peak is a ~1–2 s power-on transient
Chassis mount sheds heat to enclosure → 50 VA rating sits easy

A 50 VA toroid (~0.5 kg) is too heavy to hang on PCB pins, so it is chassis-mounted (Section 08). The two 9 V windings wire in series with the centre point grounded → ±9 Vac CT, which pm6V_TEC rectifies to ±raw. Sustained load is the steady-state hold (~1–2 A) with brief 3–5 A excursions; mounted to the chassis the 50 VA / 2.78 A-per-winding rating covers this with margin and tolerates the short peaks.

Inrush · SL22 10005 Primary-side NTC limiter — one device, both transformers 10 Ω · 90 J · 570-1032-ND ▶

Inrush limiting goes on the primary (mains) side, not the DC side. Two reasons specific to this supply: (1) the dominant surge is the toroid magnetizing inrush (10–20× rated at a zero-crossing switch-on), which only a primary-side limiter can address; (2) a fixed series resistor on the DC raw rail would carry continuous load current and eat the ±6 V dropout headroom. A DC-side resistor must in any case sit before the bank to limit it at all — but the primary NTC is the better location.

Placement: series with the line (hot) conductor, after fuse F1 and the switch, feeding both primaries (shared mains node). One device covers both transformers — which is why the larger 22 mm / 90 J part is used rather than one sized for a single small transformer.

⚠ Low-current caveat: at 0.62 A you're only ~12% of the 5 A rating, so the NTC won't self-heat to its minimum — it settles at a few ohms (~1–2 V drop, ~1 W continuous, runs warm). Negligible on a 120 V primary and on the secondary headroom. For zero steady penalty, the alternative is a relay-bypassed resistor (resistor limits the surge for a few hundred ms, then a relay shorts it out). The NTC is simpler and is the recommended choice here.
ℹ Note: the existing MOSFET soft-start (Q3/Q4) sits downstream of the raw bank, so it does not limit the bank's charge surge — the primary NTC is what protects against that. If a DC-side soft-start is preferred instead, reposition it so the raw bank charges through it.
Interface · MSTB 2,5 Power-board → main-board connector (pluggable screw, 5.08 mm) 2 blocks · split grounds ▶

Wire / drop: ±6 V_TEC and its return use single 12 AWG or 2× 16 AWG across the paralleled pins; keep the harness short so the 5 A round-trip drop stays well under the ~1.3 V H-bridge headroom (target tens of mV). The 12 A/position rating is huge margin — the parallel pins are for low resistance, not capacity.

✓ Ground discipline: carry two separate returns — GND_PWR (Block A) and GND_TEC (Block B) — and join them only at a single star point on the power board / chassis stud. The switching TEC return must not share copper with the ±18 V/analog reference. This is the reason for two blocks rather than one 9-position part.
⚠ Verify on the main board: with the H-bridge and its OPA551 error amp now on the main board fed by two arriving grounds, confirm the TEC current-sense and error-amp ground reference the TEC return (GND_TEC) appropriately, not the analog reference.
