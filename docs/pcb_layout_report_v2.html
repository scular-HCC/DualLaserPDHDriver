Special routing rules

⚡ Mains isolation & creepage
Enforce 8 mm clearance and creepage on all Mains-class nets as required by IEC 62368-1 for Pollution Degree 2, Overvoltage Category II at 250 Vac.
  • Add a routed PCB slot (0.8 mm drill, milled) between F1/D1/T1 and the first bulk cap ground to provide guaranteed creepage
  • Earth and AGND/PGND must connect at a single star point only — typically a 0 Ω resistor or ferrite bead at the C3/C4 bulk cap ground return
  • Never route low-voltage signals across or under the mains area
  • Keep the board edge clear of mains copper by > 8 mm
∿ Kelvin 4-terminal current sense
R61, R64 (TEC 0.1 Ω) and R79, R80 (laser 0.25 Ω) use Kelvin footprints. The force and sense tracks must be completely independent, joining only at the resistor pads:
  • Force tracks: TEC_Power (2.5 mm) through the resistor body
  • Sense tracks: Sensitive (0.2 mm) connected exactly at the pad
  • Route sense pair as a differential pair with < 0.2 mm spacing between them
  • Never tee the sense track off the force track — this introduces the resistance of the force copper into the sense reading
  • Keep sense tracks away from H-bridge switching edges (Q9–Q16 gates)
◈ TIA input guard rings (OPA380)
U_TIA1–U_TIA6 inputs (MPD1_K, MPD2_K, Net-(U_TIA*-IN-)) are transimpedance amplifier summing nodes carrying nanoamp-level currents.
  • Keep all copper paths from photodiode to IN– pin under 5 mm
  • Draw a copper guard ring (flood, AGND) fully encircling the IN– pin and feedback network (R_f, C_f); connects to OPA380 pin 5 if configured
  • Never route SPI, clock, or H-bridge switching tracks on the same layer adjacent to these nodes
  • Feedback caps C_f1–C_f4 (2.2 pF) must sit directly pin-to-pin — any stray trace capacitance degrades bandwidth
  • Prefer routing the TIA feedback on the opposite copper layer from the supply rails
⟳ AGND / PGND star point
The board uses split ground planes (AGND left, PGND right). Correct topology is a single-point join:
  • AGND and PGND connect only at the input bulk cap ground return (near C3/C4, Zone B)
  • Use a 0 Ω SMD resistor or ferrite bead (e.g. BLM21AG601SN1) at the star point so the connection can be severed if needed during debug
  • Earth chassis ground connects to the star point via a Y-capacitor (10 nF/400 V) — this provides HF shielding while maintaining DC isolation from earth
  • All analogue opamp returns route to AGND; all H-bridge and digital returns route to PGND
  • Do not allow PGND to "bleed" under Zone E (readback) or Zone A analog sections
≈ 10 MHz clock distribution
MCLK1, MCLK2, LOCK1_IN, Net-(J3A-In1) from J3 BNC to U36 CDCE913.
  • At 10 MHz (λ = 30 m) impedance matching is not critical, but keep runs under 50 mm
  • Use 0.25 mm (Signal class) — no wider required at this frequency
  • Route away from LASER1_A/K, LASER2_A/K — laser current transients couple easily into clock
  • BNC shield (J3 ground) should connect to a single AGND via directly at the connector body — no shared path with H-bridge return current
  • U36 CDCE913 fanout outputs to Teensy can use the same 0.25 mm class; terminate with 50–100 Ω at the receiver if runs exceed 20 mm
⊕ Laser diode routing
LASER1_A/K, BTF1_LASER/K and their channel-2 equivalents carry up to 100 mA at low voltage (V_f ≈ 1.8 V). The laser is permanently damage-prone — polarity errors or transients are fatal.
  • Route anode and cathode as a differential pair at 0.4 mm, 0.2 mm pair spacing (Laser_Drive class)
  • BAT54 clamps (D22, D23) must be placed within 3 mm of the butterfly connector pins — trace between clamp and pin must use Laser_Drive width
  • The 47 Ω protection resistors (DFBLaser R68–R89 block) limit ESD inrush — their traces to the connector pads must be Laser_Drive or narrower, not Power
  • Never route a laser track across an H-bridge switching net — inductive kick coupling can reverse-bias the laser junction
  • Silkscreen label polarity (A/K) adjacent to J_BTF1 and J_BTF2

Vias in parallel — layer transitions for high-current rails

A via is a drilled hole plated with a thin copper barrel — typically 35 µm of copper on the inside wall of a 1 mm hole. That barrel has real resistance (roughly 3–5 mΩ) and inductance (roughly 1 nH) because the barrel is narrow and long relative to a wide flat trace. When you carry a high-current rail from F.Cu to B.Cu, a single via becomes a current bottleneck. Placing two or three vias side-by-side in parallel divides both problems by N — three vias share the current equally at I/3 each, so the combined resistance drops to R/3 and inductance to L/3.

In KiCad, place two or three vias within a few millimetres of each other on the same net. You can either fan the track out with a short branch to each via, or simply drop them in a tight cluster — the router will connect them through the copper pour. The DRC won't flag them; it treats them as normal vias on the same net.
